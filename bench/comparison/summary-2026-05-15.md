- **Wrong** = the tool rewrote a site to the wrong target (e.g. `const` where

## Discussion (for the paper draft)

### var-to-const-let

- **ESLint wins coverage and safety.** Stock `prefer-const` + `no-var` rewrote
  all 126 sites correctly and produced output that compiles and passes tests.
  ESLint's `prefer-const` is a precise data-flow rule; this transform is
  squarely in its wheelhouse. ESLint's process exit code is `1` on this fixture
  — not a crash, just ESLint reporting the two `no-var` sites it deliberately
  refused to auto-fix (the `f08.ts` TDZ-trap vars). The checker correctly
  credits those two as `correct` because leaving them as `var` is the safe
  outcome we planted.
- **Refactron is the only *other* safe cell, but covers just 27%.** Refactron's
  `var_to_const_let` transform proposed changes for only 3 of 10 files
  (`f01`, `f02`, `f07`) even though its analyzer *finds* var sites in all 10.
  The transform applies whole-file and conservatively; it skips a file unless
  it can fully satisfy its preconditions. Result: every site it *did* touch was
  correct (0 wrong, 0 broken) and the output passed the verification gate — but
  73% of sites were left untouched. **This is the paper's "Refactron loses on
  coverage" outcome, and it is genuine.** It should be flagged as a transform
  that needs broadening before the comparison is published as a Refactron win.
- **jscodeshift and Comby are faster but unsafe.** Both cover ~46-48% but fail
  the safety gate. jscodeshift's hand-written reassignment scan misses `++` on
  loop counters in `for`-headers, so it emits `const i` for a mutated `i`
  (`TS2588`). Comby has no scope analysis at all — it blanket-rewrites every
  `var` to `let`, which is 66 `wrong` cells (every `const` site) and breaks
  `f08.ts`'s TDZ trap (`var`-hoisting reference before declaration becomes a
  `let` TDZ error). Speed: Comby ~0.33s, jscodeshift ~0.64s, ESLint ~0.80s,
  Refactron ~2.8s. Refactron is the slowest because of its 3-gate verification
  (syntax + imports + tests, ~3.4s of test execution) — the expected cost of
  the safety guarantee.

### format-to-fstring

- **Refactron wins coverage and safety** (75%, compiles + tests pass). It
  rewrites both `.format()` and `%`-formatting; the 23 `missed` are the
  genuinely hard kwarg / star-arg / `%(name)s` cases the fixtures planted as
  edge cases, and the 4 `wrong` are sites the bench marked `skip` that
  Refactron rewrote anyway (worth a follow-up look, but the output still
  compiles and passes tests).
- **LibCST is safe but covers 52%.** Instagram's reference
  `ConvertFormatStringCommand` rewrites `.format()` only — it does not touch
  `%`-formatting at all, which is exactly half the planted sites. Within its
  scope it is clean: 0 broken, safe output. It is also the fastest of the
  three (~2.7s vs Refactron ~4.1s).
- **Comby is the cautionary cell:** 16% coverage with **74 broken-syntax
  sites**. Comby's `:[hole]` template language cannot disambiguate
  multi-placeholder strings — `"{}={}".format(k, v)` matches such that the
  rewrite produces `f"{k, v}={}"`, which is invalid Python. The file no longer
  parses, so `py_compile` fails and the whole cell is unsafe. This is the
  clearest demonstration in the bench of why structural-text rewriting without
  a real AST is dangerous for non-trivial transforms.

### Headline

The expected shape from the methodology plan mostly holds: **Refactron and the
stock analyzers (ESLint, LibCST) are the safe cells; the flexible structural
tools (jscodeshift, Comby) are faster but produce build-breaking output.** The
honest surprise is `var-to-const-let`: **ESLint beats Refactron outright** on
both coverage and safety there, and Refactron's 27% coverage shows its
`var_to_const_let` transform is too conservative to headline a comparison win.
The paper should report this plainly — and ideally the transform is broadened
before publication.
